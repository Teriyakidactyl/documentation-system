# Task-Relative Information Presentation: Independent Evidence for Separating What Users Need *Now*

## Executive synthesis

Across information science, cognitive psychology, human factors, technical communication, decision theory, troubleshooting, and autonomous-agent research, there is unusually strong convergence on one proposition: **the best representation of information depends not merely on the information itself, but on what the user is trying to do with it at that moment**. The strongest literatures go further. They identify pairs of needs for which a single undifferentiated representation is predictably inferior because the needs demand different selection criteria, levels of abstraction, sequencing, salience, or organization. Ecological Interface Design, for example, explicitly aims not to force operators into a higher mode of cognitive processing than a task requires while still supporting qualitatively different skill-, rule-, and knowledge-based modes; technical-documentation systems independently separate learning, task execution, reference lookup, and conceptual explanation; alarm-management standards separate interruptive alarms from non-alarm information precisely to keep the alarm channel actionable. citeturn18view3turn11view0turn18view2

The evidence does **not** support a simplistic “always separate information” rule. A second, equally important result is that some information should be tightly integrated. Split-attention research shows that mutually dependent sources can impose a cost when users must mentally reunite them, while alarm management, documentation practice, and cognitive-fit research show that indiscriminately combining information with different jobs produces clutter, search cost, interference, or the wrong cognitive transformation. The deeper rule is therefore **functional integration**: integrate items needed for the *same immediate inference or action*; preserve boundaries between information serving materially different cognitive operations. A 2018 meta-analysis specifically synthesized spatial-contiguity and split-attention effects in multimedia learning, while the technical-communication and human-factors traditions independently arrive at similarly task-relative prescriptions. citeturn18view1turn15search11turn24view1turn18view2

The strongest recurring distinctions are these:

| Immediate need A | Immediate need B | Why one representation becomes difficult |
|---|---|---|
| **Execute a task now** | **Learn how the domain works** | Execution rewards brevity, branching, exception handling, and assumption of competence; learning rewards controlled sequencing, explicit basics, and managed experience. citeturn24view0turn17search0 |
| **Retrieve an exact fact** | **See a pattern or relationship** | Symbolic/tabular and spatial/graphical representations support different operations; converting between representation and required operation creates mental work. citeturn16search14turn17search29turn2search8 |
| **Interrupt for action** | **Maintain background awareness or provide information** | Making ordinary information alarm-like increases alarm load and weakens the selectivity of the interrupt channel. citeturn18view2turn17search43 |
| **Operate routinely** | **Diagnose an unfamiliar condition** | Automatic/perceptual control, rule following, and knowledge-based reasoning require different interface support and abstraction. citeturn18view3 |
| **Infer what is wrong** | **Choose what to do next** | Posterior probability and action value are different optimization targets; the most probable fault need not determine the best repair/test/action. citeturn15academia13turn15academia19 |
| **Find a known target** | **Explore an ill-defined information space** | Lookup benefits from convergence and minimal interaction; exploration benefits from alternatives, relationships, trails, and evolving mental models. citeturn21academia37turn21academia35 |
| **Get to the destination efficiently** | **Learn the environment for later independent navigation** | Turn-by-turn guidance reduces immediate navigational burden but can reduce active spatial learning. citeturn20search2turn20search20 |
| **Consult reference while working** | **Develop conceptual understanding** | Reference benefits from austere completeness and predictable structure; explanation benefits from causal narrative and discursive connections. Mixing them can obstruct both. citeturn24view1turn12search14 |
| **Use immediately relevant context** | **Preserve broad/global context** | Larger contexts can improve coverage but make retrieval less reliable; LLM research finds systematic positional retrieval failures in long contexts. citeturn22academia34 |
| **Reason about a plan** | **Act and acquire new environmental evidence** | Autonomous-agent work finds value in keeping reasoning and environmental action distinct but interleaved rather than collapsing them into one static representation. citeturn22academia33 |

A useful way to unify these results is to say that representations are optimized along at least seven latent dimensions: **time horizon** (act now versus learn for later), **epistemic objective** (retrieve, understand, diagnose, explore), **control objective** (infer versus act), **abstraction level** (raw/exact versus relational/systemic), **urgency** (interrupt versus inspect), **uncertainty** (known target versus evolving target), and **processing mode** (routine recognition versus deliberate reasoning). The apparent “presentation conflict” occurs when one representation is asked to optimize opposite ends of one or more of these dimensions. This synthesis is an inference across the independently developed literatures below rather than a taxonomy asserted by any single source. citeturn18view3turn24view0turn24view1turn18view2turn15academia13turn21academia37

## The strongest independently developed distinctions

### Learning versus immediate execution

This is one of the clearest and most independently replicated distinctions. In cognitive psychology, Soderstrom and Bjork's integrative review is explicitly organized around the difference between **performance during acquisition** and **durable learning**: conditions that make current performance look good are not necessarily the conditions that produce the best later retention or transfer. The literature therefore warns against treating immediate fluency as an adequate proxy for having learned something. citeturn17search0turn17search20turn17search42

Technical communication independently reaches essentially the same distinction in Diátaxis. It separates a **tutorial**, whose obligation is to create a successful learning experience, from a **how-to guide**, whose obligation is to help an already competent user accomplish a real task. The consequences are unusually concrete: a tutorial uses a managed, predominantly linear path, makes basic actions explicit, controls the environment, suppresses unnecessary alternatives, and deliberately exposes the learner to experiences from which competence can form. A how-to guide assumes competence, accommodates branching and exceptions, and is organized around successful completion in an uncontrolled real-world situation. Diátaxis argues that conflating the two is harmful precisely because the two purposes impose different document structures. citeturn24view0

That distinction is not unique to Diátaxis. The OASIS DITA standard institutionalizes separate information types including **concept**, **task**, **reference**, and **troubleshooting** topics rather than assuming that one generic prose form optimally serves all documentation purposes. DITA is an established OASIS standard rather than merely a writing-style recommendation, making this an example of the distinction becoming mature professional information architecture. citeturn12search6turn12search14

Navigation experiments provide a striking empirical instance. Traditional turn-by-turn GPS is extremely good at the immediate operational objective—getting someone through a route with little need to decide where to go—but work on GPS-assisted navigation reports poorer acquisition of spatial knowledge and cognitive maps. Clemenson and colleagues explicitly describe conventional turn-by-turn navigation as promoting relatively passive navigation and poorer spatial learning, and their alternative auditory-compass design deliberately withholds explicit turns so that users must remain engaged with spatial structure. citeturn20search2turn20search20

**Representation consequence.** “Do this now” information should normally foreground the next decision, preconditions, branches, checks, and exceptions; material whose primary function is to teach underlying concepts should not be inserted indiscriminately into that critical path. Conversely, a learning representation should not optimize away every difficulty, decision, or relation the learner needs to construct for themselves. This is why a procedure and a tutorial can contain the same domain facts yet properly have different architectures. citeturn24view0turn17search0

**Memoryless-agent verdict: conditional survival.** The *learning-for-later* half substantially weakens for an agent that truly cannot retain task-specific knowledge across episodes: there is little reason to optimize an experience for durable skill acquisition if nothing can be durably acquired. But the distinction does not disappear completely. During a single episode, an agent can still need either a compact action prescription or a representation that supports constructing an intermediate model from which later steps in that same episode follow. ReAct, for example, maintains reasoning traces that update an evolving action plan as environmental observations arrive. Thus “training for future reuse” disappears, while “construct a model before acting” can remain. citeturn22academia33

### Exact retrieval versus relational or pattern perception

The graph-versus-table literature produced another task-relative theory independently of technical documentation. Vessey's **cognitive fit** account treats performance as a function of fit between the representation and the operations a task requires: spatial presentations fit tasks involving relationships and patterns; symbolic presentations fit tasks involving discrete values and symbolic operations. Later work continues to describe cognitive fit in these terms, and the original graph/table literature documented performance differences rather than a universal superiority of either format. citeturn16search14turn17search1turn17search29

The importance of this result is that **“show all the same data both ways” is not automatically optimal**. A table gives direct addressability to a specific cell and preserves exact values with little decoding. A graph makes magnitude, direction, clusters, exceptions, and relations perceptually available, often at the cost of exact-value retrieval. Classic display experiments reported task- and criterion-dependent tradeoffs—for example, tabular and graphical reports differing in decision quality and speed—rather than a single display winning under all conditions. citeturn2search8turn17search17

The underlying variable is not primarily “visual versus textual.” It is **the transformation required between what the representation makes explicit and what the task asks the user to compute**. A representation is cognitively well fitted when the needed operation is close to the structure already externalized in the display; it is poorly fitted when the user must mentally reorganize it. citeturn16search14turn17search29

**Representation consequence.** Exact lookup favors stable labels, indexing, sortable rows, units adjacent to values, and low ambiguity. Trend or relationship discovery favors spatial position, grouping, ordering, visual comparison, and suppression of precision that does not help the intended perceptual judgment. A system that must support both is better served by **coordinated views or progressive disclosure** than by forcing every exact annotation onto the pattern display or every relational cue into a dense table; that design recommendation is an inference from cognitive-fit results rather than a literal prescription in Vessey's theory. citeturn16search14turn2search8

**Memoryless-agent verdict: strong survival.** This distinction does not require long-term human memory. It arises from computational fit between representation and operation. A memoryless software agent can likewise find a key-value structure ideal for exact retrieval but a relational graph more convenient for path, dependency, or neighborhood reasoning. The human perceptual mechanism will not transfer literally, but the more abstract issue—how many transformations are required to answer the current query—remains.

### Alerting versus informing, monitoring, and diagnosing

Industrial alarm management provides perhaps the clearest professional example of **deliberately refusing to make all relevant information equally salient**. The ISA-18 series treats alarm systems as a distinct lifecycle and requires prioritization, rationalization, performance monitoring, and human-machine-interface design. ISA's public guidance explicitly identifies alarm overload and nuisance alarms as problems and separately defines guidance for **non-alarm notifications**, whose purpose is to communicate non-critical information without contaminating the alarm channel. citeturn18view2

That separation exists because an alarm is not just information about the plant. Its operational semantics are approximately: **an abnormal condition demanding timely operator awareness and response**. Making ordinary status, advisory, maintenance, and informational events look and behave like alarms spends a scarce resource—the operator's interrupt attention. ISA therefore emphasizes that alarms be meaningful, prioritized, and actionable, and states that minimizing nuisance alarms and floods reduces the danger that critical alarms will be missed or ignored. citeturn18view2

Clinical alarm research reports the same human consequence in a different domain. Surveys of clinical alarm practice describe alarm fatigue as desensitization that can produce missed alarms or delayed response. The point is not simply that “too many things on screen are bad”; it is that **the interruptive representation itself loses meaning when it contains events that do not warrant interruption**. citeturn17search43

The conflict with monitoring and diagnosis is structural. An alarm channel wants **sparsity, prioritization, exceptional salience, temporal immediacy, and an implied action requirement**. A monitoring display needs persistent context, trends, normal-state information, history, and simultaneous relationships. A diagnostic display may need even more context and hypotheses. Flattening all three into one stream either makes the monitor too sparse to diagnose from or makes the alarm channel too dense to interrupt reliably. ISA's explicit distinction between alarms and informational notifications is strong professional evidence that this is not merely aesthetic preference. citeturn18view2

**Memoryless-agent verdict: very strong survival.** Durable memory is almost irrelevant. Any resource-bounded agent that must decide what events should preempt its current activity needs a distinction between **interrupt-worthy state** and **available context**. Indeed, an agent lacking durable memory may need the boundary even more, because repeatedly feeding every event into its active context consumes a finite working-context budget.

### Routine control versus rule use versus unfamiliar problem solving

Rasmussen's skill-rule-knowledge tradition and Vicente and Rasmussen's Ecological Interface Design developed an explicitly mode-sensitive theory of operational displays. EID is based on the distinction among skill-, rule-, and knowledge-based cognitive control, with two stated goals: do not force processing to a higher level than the task requires, and support all three levels when needed. The framework therefore rejects the assumption that “the maximally informative representation” is one that makes users consciously reason about everything. citeturn18view3turn7search2

This produces a subtle form of incompatibility. Routine control benefits when relevant constraints and affordances can be perceived and acted on with little symbolic reasoning. Rule-based activity needs recognizable cues linked to procedures. Novel faults require a deeper model of functional relationships and system constraints from which operators can reason when stored rules fail. An interface containing only low-level signals can make unfamiliar diagnosis impossible; an interface that forces every routine action through abstract symbolic reasoning imposes unnecessary workload. EID's stated purpose is precisely to avoid the latter while preserving support for the former. citeturn18view3

EID is important because it shows that the solution is **not necessarily separate screens**. Some conflicts can be handled with a layered representation in which different relations are perceptually available at different abstraction levels. What must remain distinct is the informational role: immediate control affordances should not be obscured by the abstractions needed only for novel analysis, while deeper constraints must remain accessible when the routine mapping ceases to work. citeturn18view3

**Memoryless-agent verdict: very strong survival.** This distinction is more general than human memory. Autonomous systems also distinguish cheap/reactive mappings from deliberative planning. The question “can the present situation be handled by an established policy, or does it require model-based reasoning?” survives even if no task-specific information is retained after the episode. The cost difference between reactive execution and deliberate search remains.

### Diagnosis versus repair or action selection

Decision-theoretic troubleshooting provides one of the cleanest cases where the two information needs are **mathematically different**, not merely psychologically different. Traditional Bayesian diagnosis updates beliefs about which faults are present. Breese and Heckerman's decision-theoretic troubleshooting framework explicitly extends that paradigm to choosing among observations, repairs, and reconfiguration actions. The decision problem therefore requires more than a ranked fault list: it must take account of what actions can be performed and their consequences. citeturn15academia13

The key implication is that **the information needed to know what is probably wrong is not identical to the information needed to know what to do next**. A diagnostic representation optimizes discrimination among hypotheses: symptoms, likelihoods, causal dependencies, tests, and posterior probabilities. An action representation must additionally encode costs, utilities, repair effects, test costs, persistence, and the value of information. The most likely fault does not automatically identify the best first action: a cheaper repair, a high-value discriminating test, or an action covering several plausible faults can rationally dominate. That conclusion follows directly from the move from belief updating to decision-theoretic action selection. citeturn15academia13turn15academia19

This distinction also clarifies why many troubleshooting interfaces feel awkward when they merge “possible causes” and “recommended steps” into one undifferentiated ranking. **Probability order is a diagnostic order; expected-value order is an action order.** They can coincide, but there is no general reason they must. Presenting one as the other discards a decision variable. citeturn15academia13

**Representation consequence.** A diagnosis pane should expose evidence, competing hypotheses, confidence, and tests that discriminate among them. A repair/action pane should expose recommended action, expected outcome, cost/risk, preconditions, and perhaps why that action is worthwhile under current uncertainty. The two should be linked—selecting an action can reveal the beliefs that justify it—but not conflated into a single “confidence” score.

**Memoryless-agent verdict: maximal survival.** This is fundamentally independent of durable memory. A one-shot agent with perfect current observations still has a belief-estimation problem and an action-selection problem. The distinction follows from probability versus utility. Of all the distinctions in this survey, this is among the least dependent on specifically human cognition. citeturn15academia13turn15academia19

### Known-target retrieval versus exploratory sensemaking

Information-retrieval research distinguishes **lookup** from **exploratory search** because the user's epistemic state is different. In lookup, the target is sufficiently well specified that success means converging on the item. In exploratory search, the user may not yet know the right vocabulary, relevant dimensions, or even exactly what a satisfactory answer will look like. Recent work operationalizes lookup versus exploratory intent as different search classes precisely because identifying the distinction can improve results and query suggestions. citeturn21academia37

Exploratory-search interfaces accordingly externalize more structure. The GRAFS work, for example, treats exploratory literature search as construction of a mental model of an unfamiliar information space; its visualization organizes concepts and relationships across search results. In a user study, the graphical concept-oriented approach improved users' recognition of relationships between key concepts compared with a conventional faceted baseline while retaining comparable basic usability. citeturn21academia35

The representational conflict is straightforward. A good known-item result can be almost aggressively convergent: a short answer, exact match, single canonical record, and minimal alternatives. That same interface is poor at showing the shape of an unfamiliar space. An exploratory representation needs neighborhoods, facets, related concepts, alternatives, provenance, query reformulation, and often persistent history. Adding all of those to every lookup imposes navigational cost; removing them from exploration makes it difficult to learn what one should search for next. citeturn21academia37turn21academia35

**Memoryless-agent verdict: strong survival, with externalization required.** An agent can enter a search with either a highly specified target or high uncertainty regardless of whether it can remember past tasks. But exploration intrinsically creates state—the discovered vocabulary, rejected branches, accumulated evidence, unresolved questions. A truly memoryless agent therefore needs that state externalized in a scratchpad, search graph, retrieval store, or environment. Without such externalization, the distinction survives but the agent is systematically disadvantaged on the exploratory side.

### Reference lookup versus conceptual explanation

Diátaxis independently separates two *non-procedural* information needs: **reference** and **explanation**. Both convey knowledge rather than action steps, but reference supports application of existing knowledge while the user is working; explanation supports acquiring a richer understanding away from the immediate work. citeturn24view1

The resulting writing structures are nearly opposites. Reference naturally gravitates toward lists, tables, signatures, exhaustive parameter descriptions, stable headings, and predictable organization. Explanation benefits from connections, causes, context, alternatives, examples, history, and sustained argument. Diátaxis explicitly warns that expanding reference entries into explanatory digressions damages the reference by interrupting lookup while simultaneously preventing the explanation from developing coherently. citeturn24view1

This is reinforced by DITA's separate **concept** and **reference** information types, alongside task and troubleshooting topics. The convergence matters: Diátaxis is a user-needs framework developed in documentation practice, whereas DITA is a formal structured-authoring standard. Both preserve a boundary between “tell me exactly what this thing is/accepts” and “help me understand how or why this part of the system fits together.” citeturn12search6turn12search14turn24view2

**Memoryless-agent verdict: moderate-to-strong survival.** If “explanation” exists only to change a durable human mental model, its value decreases for a memoryless agent. But explanations also support **present-episode causal inference, transfer, counterfactual reasoning, and choosing between alternatives**, whereas reference supports exact retrieval. Those are different computations even without persistence. The durable-learning rationale disappears; the retrieval-versus-relational-reasoning rationale remains.

### Complementary integration versus indiscriminate combination

Cognitive-load research supplies an important qualification to all the separation arguments. When two representations contain pieces that are **mutually necessary for one inference**, spatially separating them can create split attention: the user must search back and forth, hold correspondences in working memory, and mentally integrate information that the representation could have integrated externally. A 2018 meta-analysis specifically synthesized spatial contiguity and split-attention effects in multimedia environments, building on decades of experiments in which integration of mutually referring diagrams, labels, and explanatory material was compared with separated arrangements. citeturn18view1turn15search11

The implication is a more precise rule than either “combine” or “separate”:

> **Combine information whose joint use constitutes one immediate cognitive operation; separate information whose purposes, timing, or selection criteria differ.**

A formula needed to interpret a particular part of a diagram should normally be adjacent to that part; forcing visual search serves no competing purpose. By contrast, a full conceptual essay inserted into an API parameter table, a low-priority notification placed into the alarm stream, or training exposition inserted into a time-critical checklist adds material whose function is different from the current operation. The first case removes an unnecessary integration step; the latter cases introduce competing operations. This distinction is a synthesis of split-attention evidence with documentation and alarm practice. citeturn18view1turn24view1turn18view2

**Memoryless-agent verdict: strong survival.** Human working-memory mechanisms do not map literally onto an AI agent, but agents have finite context, retrieval, attention, and computation budgets. The analogous question—whether required pieces are co-located versus whether irrelevant pieces compete for processing—remains meaningful, and current LLM research provides direct evidence that sheer inclusion of relevant information in a long context does not guarantee reliable access to it. citeturn22academia34

## Where the evidence shows actual incompatibility rather than mere preference

The literatures differ substantially in evidential strength. Some establish merely that users *prefer* different forms; those are less important here. The most persuasive cases are ones in which optimizing for need A either worsens need B or forces a representational commitment that works against B.

**Immediate guidance versus later spatial knowledge** is one of the cleanest behavioral tradeoffs. Conventional GPS can reduce the user's need to make navigational decisions while simultaneously reducing active encoding of environmental structure. The alternative designs studied by Clemenson and colleagues intentionally make immediate navigation somewhat more participatory so that spatial learning is preserved. This is exactly the requested pattern: making a representation better at outsourcing the current decision can make it worse at supporting the internal model required later. citeturn20search2turn20search20

**Current performance versus learning** is broader and more mature. Soderstrom and Bjork's review exists because observable performance during practice and durable learning are dissociable outcomes. A presentation or instructional method that makes the current attempt easy can therefore be misleading as an indicator of what will be available later. This provides theoretical backing for the technical writer's distinction between a guide that ensures task completion and an instructional sequence that is deliberately constructed for learning. citeturn17search0turn24view0

**Alarm salience versus informational coverage** is a direct operational conflict. An effective alarm system cannot simply maximize the amount of abnormal or potentially relevant information that interrupts the operator; ISA's lifecycle explicitly includes rationalization, prioritization, dynamic management, performance metrics, and separate treatment of non-alarm notifications. Clinical alarm research supplies the behavioral failure mode: desensitization and delayed or missed responses. More “warning-like information” can therefore make the warning system less effective. citeturn18view2turn17search43

**Reference completeness versus explanatory continuity** is a structural conflict rather than primarily an experimental one. Reference needs stable addressability: users should be able to jump directly to an item and get its specification without traversing an argument. Explanation gains value from sustained context and relationships. Diátaxis explicitly notes that explanatory digressions obscure reference material and, when reduced to fragments inside reference, also fail to develop as proper explanation. citeturn24view1

**Routine immediacy versus novel-system reasoning** is the corresponding human-factors conflict. EID's first stated objective—not forcing processing to a higher level than task demands—would make little sense if maximum abstraction and explanation were always harmless. It recognizes that a representation ideal for deliberate knowledge-based reasoning can unnecessarily increase processing demands during routine activity, while a purely routine display leaves operators unsupported when the memorized mapping no longer applies. citeturn18view3

**Diagnosis versus intervention** is a normative incompatibility. Belief accuracy is optimized by posterior inference; intervention is optimized by expected consequences. They are related but non-identical objective functions. Decision-theoretic troubleshooting therefore has to add action costs, effects, and information value to ordinary probabilistic diagnosis. No amount of cosmetic formatting can eliminate this distinction because it originates in what the two computations optimize. citeturn15academia13turn15academia19

**Lookup versus exploration** creates opposite pressures on breadth. Lookup seeks to collapse uncertainty quickly; exploration needs to expose enough adjacent structure that the user can revise the question itself. The GRAFS user study is representative: showing conceptual relationships is valuable specifically because understanding an unfamiliar information space is part of the task, not merely finding documents matching an already stable query. citeturn21academia35turn21academia37

Finally, **large-context completeness versus reliable access** supplies an important machine analogue. Work on the “lost in the middle” phenomenon reports that LLM retrieval performance can vary sharply with where relevant material occurs in a long context rather than remaining uniform simply because everything fits within the nominal context window. A 2025 study goes further by analyzing this as an emergent consequence of competing retrieval demands. For an autonomous agent, “put all potentially relevant information in one prompt” is therefore not equivalent to “make all information equally usable.” citeturn22academia34

## Mature architectures that preserve the distinctions

A notable cross-domain pattern is that mature systems generally **coordinate different representations instead of trying to invent one universal representation**.

Technical documentation provides the clearest example. Diátaxis uses two conceptual axes—practical versus theoretical material and study versus work—to produce four forms: tutorial, how-to, reference, and explanation. Its adoption across hundreds of documentation projects makes it a mature professional architecture, although its evidence base is primarily accumulated practice rather than randomized experimentation. DITA independently formalizes multiple topic types, including task, concept, reference, and troubleshooting. citeturn11view0turn24view2turn12search6turn12search14

Human-factors engineering does something analogous through **layering and selective salience**. Ecological Interface Design attempts to make information available at levels appropriate to skill-, rule-, and knowledge-based control without making operators consciously reconstruct the system for every routine action. Alarm management reserves a privileged presentation channel for events warranting prompt response while moving ordinary notifications elsewhere. Both architectures preserve the fact that users can shift needs over time without pretending that all needs should be represented identically at every instant. citeturn18view3turn18view2

Decision-theoretic troubleshooting coordinates a **belief representation** with an **action model**. The diagnostic model represents uncertainty about causes; the decision layer evaluates observations, repairs, and configuration changes in terms of their consequences. This is a particularly useful template for AI systems because the two representations remain formally connected while retaining different semantics. citeturn15academia13

Exploratory information systems similarly add structures—facets, relationships, concept maps, trails—when the user's task is to construct an understanding of a domain, while conventional retrieval can remain optimized for direct access. The empirical GRAFS result is interesting precisely because a relationship-oriented layer improves conceptual recognition without requiring that every information-retrieval task become exploratory. citeturn21academia35

Autonomous-agent research arrives at a related design through **interleaving rather than flattening**. ReAct alternates reasoning traces and environment-facing actions: reasoning is used to formulate, track, and revise plans; action supplies observations that ground and correct the reasoning. On question answering and interactive environments, the authors report advantages over baselines in which reasoning or action is less effectively coordinated. The conceptual lesson is not that reasoning and acting should be isolated; it is that they have distinct roles and should exchange information at controlled interfaces. citeturn22academia33

This suggests four recurrent architectural solutions:

1. **Separate artifacts** when the user enters qualitatively different modes: tutorial versus procedural manual, explanation versus reference. citeturn24view0turn24view1  
2. **Layered views** when multiple modes must remain simultaneously available but should not demand equal attention: routine control plus deeper process structure. citeturn18view3  
3. **Privileged channels** when one class of information must reliably interrupt another: alarms versus informational notifications. citeturn18view2  
4. **Linked models or coordinated panes** when two computations share state but optimize different objectives: diagnosis versus repair, lookup result versus exploratory neighborhood, reasoning state versus action observation. citeturn15academia13turn21academia35turn22academia33  

The common idea is **not information silos**. It is typed information: preserve enough structure that the consumer knows whether an item is a command, a specification, an explanation, an alert, a hypothesis, an observation, or a recommendation, rather than flattening those roles into a generic block of “relevant context.” This conclusion is a synthesis across the cited traditions. citeturn12search14turn18view2turn15academia13

## What survives for an agent with no durable task-specific memory

For this question, it helps to make “without durable task-specific memory” precise. Assume an agent does **not** carry learned task state into a future episode, but it can hold some current context, inspect external state, and perhaps write to an external workspace during the present episode. Under that assumption, the distinctions divide into three classes.

### Distinctions that survive essentially unchanged

**Diagnosis versus action** survives completely. Even a stateless one-shot agent must distinguish “what state is probably true?” from “which action maximizes expected value?” Probability and utility remain different quantities. citeturn15academia13turn15academia19

**Alarm versus context** also survives. A resource-bounded agent must decide which observations should preempt the current plan and which can remain available for later inspection. The underlying scarce resource becomes attention/context/computation rather than human vigilance, but the selectivity problem is structurally similar. The empirical long-context literature makes the analogy less speculative: making more material nominally available does not guarantee uniform retrieval from it. citeturn18view2turn22academia34

**Known-item lookup versus exploration** survives. It depends on uncertainty about the target, not on long-term memory. A memoryless agent can know exactly what key it seeks or be uncertain about the structure of the search space. The caveat is that exploration creates information that must persist *somewhere during the episode*, so externalized notes or state become necessary when internal persistence is weak. citeturn21academia37turn21academia35

**Routine/reactive control versus model-based problem solving** survives. The difference is computational: direct policy execution and model-based search have different costs and affordances. EID's specifically perceptual prescriptions are human-centered, but its higher-level principle—not forcing expensive reasoning when a direct mapping suffices while retaining deeper structure for exceptional cases—transfers readily. citeturn18view3

**Exact retrieval versus relational computation** survives at an abstract level. A software agent may not enjoy human graphical perception, so “graph beats table” cannot simply be copied. But the cognitive-fit principle generalizes: use a representation whose primitives correspond closely to the operation to be performed. Exact key lookup, neighborhood traversal, temporal trend detection, and causal inference naturally favor different indexes and structures. This is an extrapolation from cognitive-fit theory rather than evidence that artificial agents exhibit the same human effects. citeturn16search14turn17search29

### Distinctions that survive, but in altered form

**Reference versus explanation** survives insofar as explanation is needed for *current reasoning*. A memoryless agent has little need to read an explanation merely to enrich a durable future mental model, but it can need causal context to resolve ambiguity, derive a novel consequence, or handle an unforeseen case. Reference still serves exact retrieval. The human “study versus work” axis therefore changes into something closer to **direct retrieval versus generative inference**. citeturn24view1

**Complementary integration versus separation** likely survives through context economics rather than human visual working memory. A language agent benefits when mutually dependent premises are jointly accessible, but broad irrelevant context can reduce retrieval reliability. The lost-in-the-middle literature gives direct evidence that position and retrieval demands matter even when all information technically fits in the model's context. citeturn22academia34

**Reasoning versus acting** also survives but favors a protocol rather than permanent documents. ReAct demonstrates one such protocol: alternate thought-like state updates with actions that obtain new observations. That is exactly the architecture one would expect for a low-memory agent: preserve only the state necessary for the next inference/action transition, with the environment or an external workspace carrying the rest. citeturn22academia33

### Distinctions that substantially weaken

**Learning-oriented tutorial versus immediate procedure** weakens most. If an agent cannot retain any task-specific competence after the episode, there is little benefit in deliberately arranging the experience to create durable learning. The execution representation dominates unless the tutorial-like process builds an intermediate model needed later in the *same* episode. Human results separating current performance from later learning are therefore real but not fully agent-invariant. citeturn17search0turn24view0

**Turn-by-turn navigation versus cognitive-map acquisition** similarly weakens when no later reuse is possible. The major human cost of turn-by-turn guidance—poorer retained environmental knowledge—does not matter to an agent that will forget the environment after arrival. Yet a smaller distinction remains: a global spatial model can still improve rerouting, contingency handling, shortcut discovery, or recognizing that a local instruction is inconsistent during the same journey. Thus the long-term-learning component disappears while the **local instruction versus global state model** distinction remains. citeturn20search2turn20search20

A concise survival map is therefore:

| Distinction | Survives without durable task memory? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis ↔ action | **Yes, strongly** | Different objective functions: probability versus utility. citeturn15academia13 |
| Alarm ↔ background context | **Yes, strongly** | Interrupt policy and finite processing resources remain. citeturn18view2turn22academia34 |
| Routine control ↔ novel reasoning | **Yes, strongly** | Reactive execution and model-based search remain computationally distinct. citeturn18view3 |
| Lookup ↔ exploration | **Yes, strongly** | Determined by target uncertainty; exploration needs externally maintained state. citeturn21academia37turn21academia35 |
| Exact fact ↔ relational pattern | **Yes, abstractly** | Representation-to-operation fit remains; human visual advantages need not. citeturn16search14 |
| Reference ↔ explanation | **Partly/mostly** | Becomes direct retrieval versus current causal/inferential reasoning. citeturn24view1 |
| Integrated evidence ↔ broad context | **Yes** | Retrieval/attention bottlenecks exist in current LLMs. citeturn22academia34 |
| Tutorial ↔ how-to | **Partly** | Durable-learning objective vanishes; within-episode model building remains. citeturn24view0turn17search0 |
| Turn guidance ↔ map learning | **Partly/weakly** | Future spatial learning vanishes; global model still helps present replanning. citeturn20search2 |

The striking conclusion is that **most of the important separations do not depend on durable human memory at all**. The ones that disappear are specifically those whose second objective is *future learning*. Distinctions based on uncertainty, utility, urgency, abstraction, search topology, or computational cost remain.

## A unified design principle and practical taxonomy

The research supports a stronger rule than “personalize the amount of detail.” A presentation system should identify the **immediate operation to be supported** and select a representation whose organization corresponds to that operation. Across the reviewed traditions, at least six recurrent presentation modes emerge.

| Mode | User's immediate question | Optimize for | Suppress or defer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Execute** | “What do I do next?” | Preconditions, next step, checks, branches, exceptions | Background theory unless needed for the decision. citeturn24view0 |
| **Retrieve** | “What exactly is X?” | Exactness, stable labels, exhaustive fields, indexability | Discursive digression. citeturn24view1turn12search14 |
| **Understand** | “Why/how does this work?” | Relations, causes, comparisons, examples, conceptual continuity | Operational detail not needed for the model. citeturn24view1 |
| **Monitor/alert** | “Does something need my attention now?” | Salience, prioritization, actionability, temporal relevance | Noncritical notifications and background status. citeturn18view2 |
| **Diagnose/explore** | “What is going on?” | Competing hypotheses, evidence, uncertainty, relationships, search history | Premature commitment to one answer or action. citeturn15academia13turn21academia35 |
| **Decide/repair** | “What should I do about it?” | Actions, consequences, cost/risk, expected value, information value | Hypothesis detail that cannot change the action. citeturn15academia13turn15academia19 |

The boundary between modes should be **permeable but explicit**. A procedure can link to explanation; an alarm can open a diagnostic display; a fault hypothesis can expose the repair calculation; a search result can expand into an exploratory neighborhood. What the evidence argues against is forcing all of those structures to occupy the same attentional foreground simultaneously. Diátaxis's cross-links, ISA's separation of alarms from notifications, EID's support for multiple levels of control, and decision-theoretic troubleshooting's separate inference and action layers are all variations on this architecture. citeturn24view0turn24view1turn18view2turn18view3turn15academia13

For autonomous agents, the analogous architecture would not be “give the model the biggest possible context.” It would be **typed, need-conditioned context assembly**: maintain a broad external store; retrieve a compact action view for execution; retrieve exact reference records for lookup; construct relational evidence sets for diagnosis; maintain a separate action-value representation for decision; and externalize exploratory state when the agent lacks durable memory. ReAct provides direct evidence for coordinating rather than collapsing reasoning and acting, while long-context retrieval work shows why merely concatenating everything is not a reliable substitute for such selection. citeturn22academia33turn22academia34

The most defensible generalization from the full body of evidence is therefore:

> **Information should be organized by the operation it is meant to enable, not only by the subject matter it describes. Two needs should share a foreground representation when they require the same immediate integration; they should remain distinct when they optimize different horizons, cognitive transformations, interrupt policies, uncertainty reductions, or utility functions.**

That principle explains otherwise independent results from graphs versus tables, tutorials versus procedures, reference versus explanation, alarms versus notifications, routine versus knowledge-based control, diagnosis versus repair, lookup versus exploration, GPS guidance versus spatial learning, split-attention research, and reasoning-versus-acting agent architectures. The strongest agent-invariant distinctions are the ones grounded in **objective structure**—probability versus utility, interrupt versus background processing, exact retrieval versus relational computation, known versus uncertain target, and reactive versus deliberative control. The distinctions most dependent on specifically human durable memory—tutorial versus practice aid and route guidance versus spatial learning—are the ones most likely to collapse for a genuinely memoryless agent. citeturn15academia13turn18view2turn16search14turn21academia37turn18view3turn17search0turn20search2
