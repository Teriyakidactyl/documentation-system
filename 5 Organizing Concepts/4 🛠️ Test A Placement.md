# 🛠️ Test A Placement

Use this procedure before domain-specific placement rules when conceptual fit is
not obvious. Placement consumes upstream structural decisions; it does not
silently invent them to make a candidate fit.

## 1. Confirm the inputs

Before comparing containers, confirm that three inputs are established:

1. the candidate is one structural unit rather than an unresolved compound;
2. its governing relationship and applicable topology are known;
3. its semantic owner is known, including any relevant convergence site.

If one of these is load-bearing and unresolved, return to the corresponding
Organizing Concepts procedure instead of inferring the answer from the
available containers.

## 2. Test the receiving relation and axis

Name the relation and axis already governing each plausible receiving layer.

A placement is compatible only when the candidate can participate in that
relation without changing what either the candidate or the layer means. A
classification layer answers one classification question. An ordered layer
uses one ordering dimension. A matrix keeps independent axes independent. A
graph stores important edges explicitly.

If a candidate fits two exclusive siblings for unrelated reasons, do not choose
the nearer-looking branch. The receiving layer is mixing axes, the candidate
boundary is wrong, or an upstream relationship remains unresolved.

## 3. Judge the granularity

Give a grouping depth only when the grouping is real.

Pull an isolated leaf back up when no sibling can reasonably join it. Nest two
or more items when they share a stable relation that distinguishes the group
from its peers. In a hierarchy, a parent must remain more abstract than every
child it contains.

Do not add depth because a template has room for it, and do not keep a real
group flat merely to avoid adding structure.

## 4. Preserve ownership and convergence

The receiving file, folder, heading, object, or module is not evidence that it
owns the candidate.

Confirm that the placement preserves the established owner. When the container
differs from the owner, keep that distinction legible. When the container is a
convergence site, keep independently owned concerns independently governed
rather than transferring their rules to the coordinator.

Reject a placement that requires changing semantic ownership merely to match
the existing representation.

## 5. Check the local structure

Use the placement attempt as a probe for local overlap and gaps.

**Overlap.** A unit should not compete between exclusive siblings for
independent reasons. Revisit the unit boundary, relationship, or layer axis
rather than adding a special-case placement.

**Gap.** A unit that fits only after stretching every candidate definition
exposes missing structure. A concrete counter-example from the same domain is
enough to confirm that gap; failure to find one is evidence, not proof, that
the structure is exhaustive.

Do not force exclusivity onto a matrix or graph where combination or multiple
edges are part of the topology.

## 6. Resolve or escalate

Confirm the placement only when the established unit, relationship, owner, and
receiving structure agree.

When they do not, name the earliest contradicted structural decision and return
to it. Do not repair a downstream placement by quietly redefining an upstream
unit or relationship.

When the contradiction exposes a reusable taxonomy, schema boundary, or other
consequential structure that must be constructed or formally validated, follow
the structural decomposition and validation procedure rather than creating
precedent from a one-off exception.
