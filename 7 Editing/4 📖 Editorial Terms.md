# 📖 Editorial Terms

Use established editorial terms when their recognized scope matches the work.
Revision and Validation state what authority an operation has; the editorial
pass states the level and class of prose intervention. These axes are
independent.

The established names carry broader editorial practice with them; the local
rule below narrows their use inside Documentation System without renaming them.

| Term | Recognized scope | Documentation System rule |
|---|---|---|
| **developmental editing** | Macro-level structure, argument, coverage, sequence, logical gaps, and large-scale organization. | Treat as a Revision mandate. Validate structural and semantic state before mutation, then permit restructuring only where the frozen frame authorizes it. |
| **line editing** | Paragraph- and sentence-level clarity, flow, emphasis, rhythm, local logic, and expression. | Treat as Revision below the macro architecture. Escalate a structural or conceptual defect rather than silently turning a line edit into developmental editing. |
| **copyediting** | Correctness, consistency, grammar, terminology, cross-references, usage, and application of house style. | Split detection from correction: Validation identifies faults; Revision applies authorized corrections. State light, medium, or heavy scope when that distinction changes permitted intervention. |
| **proofreading** | Final microscopic inspection after content and structure are stable, focused on typographical, punctuation, spacing, formatting, and similar mechanical defects. | Treat as a Validation pass by default. Do not introduce structural or conceptual change during proofreading; a correction requires a separately authorized Revision step. |
| **style sheet** | Artifact-specific record of editorial decisions such as spelling, capitalization, terminology, numbering, and formatting choices. | Use as an external consistency aid and validation input. It supplements governing specifications and house style; it does not become a second source of truth for decisions already owned elsewhere. |
| **house style** | Shared editorial rules governing a class of artifacts. | Treat the applicable repository writing rules and document specification as governing frame inputs. An artifact-specific style sheet may narrow unresolved choices but may not contradict them. |

Do not invent a new editing label merely to describe a familiar editorial
scope. Introduce a local term only when the established vocabulary does not name
the distinction that the procedure must enforce, such as `checked-clean`,
`residual`, or `edit-induced fault`.
